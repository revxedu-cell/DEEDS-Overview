Phase 1 of DEEDS

Where curiosity meets consciousness.

Students see, hear, and feel a challenge before they study it, building empathy, connection, and social consciousness. This creates a compelling personal "why" for the learning and action to come.

Your Role in Discover

Teacher Guidance

Your job in this phase is to make the challenge impossible to ignore. You're designing an experience that gives students a real reason to care before you ask them to investigate anything.

  • Select an immersive experience (simulation, field trip, video, guest, data provocation) that connects the challenge to students' lives or community.
  • Identify at least one community partner or person affected by the challenge who students can hear from.
  • Draft a challenge question to launch the unit, with a plan for how students will refine it.
  • Plan structured time for students to reflect and make personal connections after the immersive experience.
  • Consider how students will document and share the Evidence of Learning. Will their articulation of the challenge form part of their solution or Share presentation?
  • Think through what this challenge might bring up for students personally. Plan how you'll create space for diverse reactions and experiences.
  • Create a visual space in the classroom to document learning throughout the challenge. This can include a word wall, project tracking board, and the Uncovering the Challenge chart.
  • Center scholar voice during the immersive experience — resist the urge to explain; let students name what they see and feel.
  • Run protocols with structure and intention, selecting scaffolds based on what your students need to access and engage with the material.
  • Guide students to co-create, refine, or internalize the challenge question.
  • Check that students can name the challenge, describe who's affected, and articulate why it matters before moving to Examine.

The continuum describes how students' ability to understand a challenge and make evidence-based claims develops over time. Explore Examples

Each example outlines a sample Discover phase to spark inspiration for what this phase might look like in your own classroom.

Grades K–2: Peaceful Paws

"How can we create calming toys that help pets feel calm while they wait to be adopted?"

Students learn that shelter dogs often feel anxious, which can prevent them from getting adopted. Over 5 sessions, they experience the challenge firsthand, examine evidence, and make a case for why solving it matters.
