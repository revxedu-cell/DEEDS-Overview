Assessment

Guidance on assessing student learning across DEEDS.

Assessment shows you what students understand, what they can do, and what they need next.

Collect evidence of three things throughout a unit:

  • standards mastery
  • application of learning
  • growth across the DEEDS process

Plan Assessment for Your Unit

Think through the following assessment components as you plan the unit.

1

Identify priority standards

Choose the knowledge and skills every student must master.

2

Define individual evidence

Decide how each student will independently demonstrate mastery. Team products show collaboration, but they don't always show what each student understands on their own.

3

Plan the final assessment and rubric

Decide how you'll assess the final solution, individual standards mastery, and student reflection on their learning. Design your standards-based rubric now, before you plan the phases — it defines what quality looks like from the start and becomes the tool students use throughout the project to self-reflect, guide their revisions, and give peer feedback.

4

Plan phase deliverables

Identify the major evidence students will produce in each phase.

5

Plan checks for understanding

Decide when you'll pause to assess learning, catch misconceptions, and adjust instruction.

6

Build in feedback and revision

Plan when students get feedback and how they'll use it to improve their work.

Assessment Across the Five Phases

Phase What to assess What to do with the evidence
Discover Can students define the challenge, explain who it affects, and show why it matters? Use gaps and questions to plan Examine.
Examine Do students deeply understand the content and skills at the level of rigor required by the standards? Can they analyze root causes and defend a solution direction? Reteach, give practice, adjust groups, or move forward.
Engineer Can students design, build, justify, test, and revise a solution? Give feedback on reasoning, functionality, craftsmanship, and readiness.
Do Can students implement the solution, analyze evidence, and evaluate its effectiveness? Help students sharpen their conclusions and identify responsible next steps.
Share Can students explain their learning, use evidence, respond to questions, and reflect on growth and impact? Assess learning and find patterns to inform the next unit.

Core Assessment Tools

Standards-Based Rubric

Design one rubric per project, tied to the priority standards and the quality of the final solution. Share it with students at the start of the project so it sets the standard for quality work from day one. Students use the same rubric throughout the project to self-reflect on their progress, guide their own revisions, and give each other peer feedback.

Standards Mastery Tracker

Track what each student can independently demonstrate on each priority standard. Log every assessment with a date, so reassessing a standard adds new evidence instead of overwriting the record — and see current status by student or by standard at a glance. Keep standards mastery separate from the quality of the team product.

Checks for Understanding

Use short, individual assessments during Examine and whenever students learn important content or skills: exit tickets, short quizzes, written responses, worked problems, diagrams, oral explanations, brief application tasks. Use the results to make an immediate instructional decision.

End-of-Phase Performance Tasks

Use one performance task at the end of each phase to decide if students are ready to move forward. Each task needs clear directions, required evidence, success criteria, and an individual reflection.

Feedback and Revision Log

Use one shared log across the unit. Students record the feedback they received, who gave it, what they changed, why they changed it, and how the revision improved their work.

Summative Assessment (Share)

At the end of the unit, assess three things separately:

  1. Final solution — Is it thoughtful, evidence-based, and responsive to the challenge?
  2. Individual mastery — Can each student independently demonstrate the priority standards?
  3. Learning and growth — Can students explain their reasoning, development, and impact?

Use Evidence to Decide What Happens Next

Assessment should drive action. Use the evidence to reteach a concept, add practice, adjust groups or scaffolds, give feedback, require revision, offer extension, reassess, or improve the next unit.

The goal isn't more grades. It's making learning visible and responding to what students need.
