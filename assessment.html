Assessment

Guidance on assessing student learning across DEEDS.

Assessment shows you what students understand, what they can do, and what they need next.

Collect evidence of three things throughout a unit:

  • standards mastery
  • application of learning
  • growth across the DEEDS process

Plan Assessment for Your Unit

Think through the following assessment components as you plan the unit.

1

Identify priority standards

Choose the knowledge and skills every student must master.

2

Define individual evidence

Decide how each student will independently demonstrate mastery. Team products show collaboration, but they don't always show what each student understands on their own.

3

Plan the final assessment and rubric

Decide how you'll assess the final solution, individual standards mastery, and student reflection on their learning. Design your standards-based rubric now, before you plan the phases — it defines what quality looks like from the start and becomes the tool students use throughout the project to self-reflect, guide their revisions, and give peer feedback.

4

Plan phase deliverables

Identify the major evidence students will produce in each phase.

5

Plan checks for understanding

Decide when you'll pause to assess learning, catch misconceptions, and adjust instruction. Use what you find to reteach a concept, add practice, adjust groups or scaffolds, give feedback, require revision, offer extension, or reassess — the goal isn't more grades, it's making learning visible and responding to what students need.

6

Build in feedback and revision

Plan when students get feedback and how they'll use it to improve their work.

Core Assessment Tools

Tool What to assess How to assess it
Standards-Based Rubric Quality of the final solution against the priority standards. Share it with students at the start of the project. Have them use the same rubric throughout to self-reflect, guide revisions, and give peer feedback.
Standards Mastery Tracker Each student's independent mastery of every priority standard. Log every assessment with a date. Reassessing a standard adds new evidence instead of overwriting the record — see current status by student or by standard.
Checks for Understanding In-the-moment understanding of content and skills, especially during Examine. Use exit tickets, short quizzes, written responses, worked problems, diagrams, oral explanations, or brief application tasks. Use results to make an immediate instructional decision.
End-of-Phase Performance Tasks Whether students are ready to move to the next phase. Give one task per phase with clear directions, required evidence, success criteria, and an individual reflection.
Developmental Continuums Growth in reasoning and skill across multiple DEEDS units — not a single project. Not a grade-level rubric. Name current strengths, give specific feedback, and make next steps visible.
