Crafting Your Learning Journey

Map what students will learn, do, and produce across all five phases, and make sure each phase sets up the next.

Unit Design

Use the Learning Journey section of the DEEDS Unit Planning Template to map what students will learn, do, and produce in each phase. Each phase should set up the next: a Discover deliverable specific enough for Examine to investigate, a solution direction from Examine that Engineer can build, a solution from Engineer that Do can put into action, and evidence from Do that Share can present.

Learning Target(s)

One or more "I can... so that..." statements naming the standards or skills students will master, framed within the challenge.

Key Learning Experiences

The activities that build knowledge and connection to the challenge. Some are core DEEDS protocols from the phase pages; others you'll design yourself. Facilitate the investigation and let students do the intellectual work.

Deliverable(s)

What students produce to show their learning. A strong deliverable demonstrates the phase's success criteria and feeds into the final solution, rather than standing alone.

The Learning Journey
Phase Learning Target(s) Key Learning Experiences Deliverable(s)
Discover Full guidance → "I can... so that..." naming what students will know or do by the end of Discover. How will students encounter the challenge firsthand and connect to it emotionally? Simulations, field trips, client interviews, or compelling data. Shows students understand the challenge and its impacts. Where possible, a component of the end product (an intro slide, an early problem definition).
Examine Full guidance → "I can" statements naming the standards and skills students need, contextualized in the challenge. How will students lead their own investigation? Labs, research, text analysis, mini lessons, or station rotations, with formative checkpoints along the way. Demonstrates core content and skills while building toward the Engineer solution: a CER-based argument, a research brief, an annotated diagram.
Engineer Full guidance → "I can" statements naming the design skills and content needed to address a root cause. How will students develop a plan, build with community partner support, and exchange feedback across iterations? The real solution carried into Do: a complete product, a finished proposal, or a working system, built to a standard students are proud to put into action.
Do Full guidance → "I can" statements naming what students need to implement and evaluate their solution. How will students implement their solution, collect data, and reflect on its effectiveness? Evidence of impact students collect and analyze, giving them something concrete to draw on in Share.
Share Full guidance → "I can" statements naming the communication skills students need to present and defend their work. How will students share with a public audience? Decide whether a summative assessment fits your unit. Shows students' growth across the full journey, not just the final phase.
