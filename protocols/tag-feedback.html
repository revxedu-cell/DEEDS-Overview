ENGINEER Feedback Protocol
⏱ 15 min
T.A.G. Feedback
Share your draft plan with another team and/or a professional mentor, then get feedback.
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
🩷
Tell Something That's Working
  • One strength of this design is…
  • This part clearly meets the criteria because…
  • I can see that you considered ___ when you…
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
❓
Ask a Question
  • Is this realistic given the materials?
  • How will this function under the stated constraints?
  • What might fail first?
  • How will this work if…?
  • What happens if…?
  • Have you considered…?
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
💡
Give a Suggestion
Offer a concrete improvement to strengthen feasibility, usability, safety, durability, or alignment with the criteria.
  • You might improve this by…
  • Consider adjusting…
  • It may be stronger if…

Teacher Background — Engineer
T.A.G. Feedback
When to use
After a draft design or plan — before building or finalizing
Standards Alignment
MS-ETS1-2
Evaluating competing design solutions — the T.A.G. structure gives students a concrete process for critiquing a peer team's design against shared criteria.
MS-ETS1-3
Analyzing data to improve a design — the Give a Suggestion step pushes reviewers toward specific, testable improvements rather than vague praise.
CCSS SL.1
Collaborative discussion — students engage in structured, evidence-based dialogue about a peer's design.
Facilitation Notes
  • Post the five criteria categories (feasibility, usability, safety, durability, alignment) visibly so Give a Suggestion stays concrete instead of generic.
  • Model one full round yourself first — reviewers often default to only praise or only critique without a demonstration.
  • Circulate during Ask a Question; this is where teams most often surface a real flaw in their plan before building.
  • Set a visible timer per round — 5 minutes goes fast, and Give a Suggestion is the round most likely to get rushed.
  • If a professional mentor is present, brief them on the protocol beforehand so their feedback follows the same T.A.G. structure as peer feedback.
Why this, why now
Ownership: Teams hear specific, criteria-based feedback on their own design before committing materials and time to building it. Catching a feasibility problem here is far cheaper than catching it mid-build.

Teacher Background — Share
T.A.G. Feedback
When to use
During rehearsal — before the final presentation
Standards Alignment
CCSS SL.1
Collaborative discussion — partners engage in structured, evidence-based dialogue about a rehearsed presentation.
CCSS SL.4
Presenting claims and findings — the protocol gives students language to evaluate whether a partner's presentation is clear and well-supported.
NGSS SEP 7
Engaging in argument from evidence — the Ask a Question round pushes presenters to defend their evidence and reasoning aloud before the real audience does.
Facilitation Notes
  • Pair students who haven't rehearsed together yet — a partner who's heard the presentation before will give thinner feedback.
  • Reference the Share Feedback Checklist explicitly before starting, so reviewers know what to listen for during the 5-minute rehearsal.
  • Keep the three feedback rounds tight — 2 minutes each. The value is in the immediate, specific reaction, not a long discussion.
  • Circulate and listen in during Ask a Question; the questions partners raise here often predict what a real audience will ask.
  • Have students jot down one piece of feedback to act on before their final presentation — the protocol should change something, not just be a rehearsal formality.
Why this, why now
Ownership: Students get low-stakes, specific feedback on their presentation from a peer before facing the real audience, so revisions happen before it counts.
