CROSS-PHASE Feedback Protocol
⏱ ~10–15 min
T.A.G. Feedback
Trade work with a partner or team. Use these three moves to give feedback that's specific and useful.
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
🩷
Tell
  • One thing I liked was…
  • You worked hard on…
›
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
❓
Ask
  • What did you make or figure out?
  • Who is this for?
›
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
💡
Give
  • You could try…
  • Maybe add…
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
🩷
Tell Something That's Working
  • One thing you explained clearly was …
  • I noticed you used evidence when…
  • I learned something about you when….
›
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
❓
Ask a Question
  • Why did you choose…?
  • What evidence helped you decide…?
  • Who does this work matter to, and how?
›
👥 Partner⏳ 2 min
💡
Give a Suggestion
  • You could make this stronger by explaining … more.
  • Consider adding…
  • Try explaining… in a simpler way.
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
🩷
Tell Something That's Working
  • One strength of this work is…
  • This part clearly meets the criteria because…
  • I can see that you considered ___ when you…
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
❓
Ask a Question
  • Is this realistic given the evidence or materials?
  • How will this hold up under questions or constraints?
  • What might not work, or what happens if…?
  • Have you considered…?
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
💡
Give a Suggestion
Offer a concrete improvement to strengthen clarity, evidence, feasibility, or alignment with the criteria.
  • You might improve this by…
  • Consider adjusting…
  • It may be stronger if…
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
🩷
Tell Something That's Working
  • One strength of your reasoning or design is…
  • You effectively grounded ___ in evidence when you…
  • This part holds up because…
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
❓
Ask a Question
  • What's the strongest counterargument or failure case here, and how would you respond?
  • What assumption is this resting on?
  • How does this hold up against a real-world constraint?
›
👥 Partner⏳ 5 min
💡
Give a Suggestion
Offer a specific improvement that would strengthen the rigor, feasibility, or persuasiveness of this work.
  • Consider addressing…
  • This would be stronger if you accounted for…
  • It may be stronger if…
Teacher Background — Cross-Phase
T.A.G. Feedback
When to use
Any point where students exchange work for peer feedback
Using This Protocol Across Phases
Engineer
Partners or a mentor review a draft design, plan, or prototype. Point Ask and Give toward the criteria and constraints: feasibility, usability, safety, durability, alignment.
Share
Partners review a rehearsed presentation before the real audience sees it. Point Ask and Give toward clarity, evidence, and audience-readiness.
Examine
Partners review a claim or root-cause analysis before it's finalized. Point Ask toward whether the evidence actually supports the claim.
Do
Partners review a draft write-up of evidence or impact. Point Give toward naming limits, tradeoffs, or what the data doesn't show.
Standards Alignment
CCSS SL.1
Collaborative discussion — students engage in structured, evidence-based dialogue about a peer's work.
MS-ETS1-2
Evaluating competing design solutions — applies when used in Engineer to critique a design against shared criteria.
CCSS SL.4
Presenting claims and findings — applies when used in Share to evaluate clarity and evidence in a presentation.
Facilitation Notes
  • Model one full round yourself first — reviewers often default to only praise or only critique without a demonstration.
  • Post the three moves (Tell, Ask, Give) visibly, and swap in the phase-specific focus from the box above before students start.
  • Set a visible timer per round so Give a Suggestion doesn't get rushed — it's the round most likely to run short.
  • Circulate during Ask a Question; the questions partners raise here often predict what a real audience, mentor, or test will ask.
  • Have students name one piece of feedback they'll act on before moving forward — the protocol should change something, not just be a formality.
Why this, why now
Ownership: Students get specific, criteria-based feedback on their own work from a peer before it counts — whether that's before building, before presenting, or before finalizing a claim. Catching a problem here is cheaper than catching it later.
