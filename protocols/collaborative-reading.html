Annotate the Text
2mRead for fluency (teacher-led)
2mRead for fluency (students only)
2mAnnotate using markings for understanding
2mDiscuss the annotations
Aligns to: Reading Anchor Standard R.1 (cite evidence from the text)
Phase 3
Key Details
2mRead for fluency (students only)
1mAnnotate the question
2mFind and cite your answer (if multiple choice)
2mExplain answers, one correct and one incorrect, with rationale
Aligns to: Reading Anchor Standards R.1, R.2 (key details and central ideas)
Phase 4
Craft and Structure
2mRead for fluency (students only)
1mHighlight the author's craft question type
3mComplete the question and explain answers
2mDiscuss the correct answer and rationale
Aligns to: Reading Anchor Standards R.4, R.5, R.6 (craft and structure)
Phase 5
Synthesize
2mRead for fluency (students only)
1mPrompt students to lead annotation
5m"Think time" with music to complete a response
5mGroup share with TAG feedback (Tell, Ask, Give)
Aligns to: Writing Anchor Standard W.9, Speaking & Listening Anchor Standard SL.1
Phase 1
Close Reading & Annotation
15–30 min, on your own
Identify the central idea and cite supporting evidence.
Mark claims, evidence, key vocabulary, and confusing parts.
Choose one unfamiliar word and define it using context clues.
Complete the CTA Organizer to prepare for discussion.
Aligns to: Reading Anchor Standards R.1, R.2, R.4
Phase 2
Discussion & Analysis
10 min, with your group
Round-robin share: central idea, a limitation, and a question or challenge.
As a group, agree on the author's purpose and perspective.
Discuss key assumptions or limitations, and how the argument could be stronger.
Aligns to: Speaking & Listening Anchor Standard SL.1, Reading Anchor Standards R.5, R.6
Phase 3
Written Response
10 min, on your own
Write a 4–6 sentence response using specific textual evidence.
Choose one lens: argument and evaluation, explanatory and criticality, identity and relevance, or comparative analysis.
Explain how the evidence supports your reasoning, going beyond summary.
Aligns to: Writing Anchor Standards W.1, W.2, Reading Anchor Standard R.9
Phase 4
Real World Application
10 min, small group then whole class
Connect: how does this text connect to your life, community, or a real-world issue?
Extend: how does this text deepen or change your thinking?
Challenge: how does this text push you to act, question, or take a stand?
One group shares a takeaway; classmates build on it as a class.
Aligns to: Speaking & Listening Anchor Standard SL.1

Why this, why now
Dr. Alexa Sorden developed the Collaborative Reading Approach in 2013 to build proficient readers through repeated choral reading, annotation, and evidence-based discussion. Reading research identifies five core skills every proficient reader needs: phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension. This routine gives students repeated, structured practice with all five in a single daily cycle.

Text Selection & Length
  • K–2: up to 150 words   3–8: up to 250 words   9–12: up to 300 words
  • Determine the text's Lexile range using the Lexile and Quantile Hub: paste in the text or excerpt to generate its range and flag words critical to understanding it.
  • For nonfiction, you can adjust the Lexile range. Add context clues (definitions, appositives, synonyms) to support unfamiliar vocabulary, and shorten long or complex sentences to bring the range down.
  • For fiction, never alter the author's words. The only allowed change is using ellipses to cut an irrelevant section. Tell students when a part has been left out, and choose excerpts that highlight a conflict, a character trait, or the story's theme.
Facilitation Notes: K–5
  • Don't cut the repeated reading, even under time pressure. The first pass builds comprehension, and the second builds fluency through repetition.
  • Model fluent reading aloud before asking K-3 students to read independently. Students need to hear proficient reading before they can produce it.
  • Treat the vocabulary step as instruction, not a formality. Vocabulary is one of the five core skills this routine is built to reinforce.
Facilitation Notes: 6–12
  • The repeated reading in Phase 1 still matters at this age. Fluency continues to affect comprehension well past elementary school, especially with unfamiliar or complex texts.
  • Keep Phase 2 discussion anchored to the text. Ask students to point to where an idea comes from, then explain how it supports their answer. That evidence-to-reasoning link is what builds comprehension.
  • Real World Application in Phase 4 is where this routine connects most directly to the DEEDS challenge. Don't skip it for time.
