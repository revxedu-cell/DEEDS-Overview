Phase 1
Close Reading & Claim Analysis
10 min, independent
Read the text closely, once, on your own.
Identify the author's central claim and annotate the evidence used to support it.
Note where the text connects to the Crosscutting Concept your teacher has named.
Aligns to selected NGSS SEP (Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information)
Phase 2
Data & Evidence Evaluation
10 min, independent or pairs
Analyze the data, model, or evidence the text presents.
Evaluate whether it's sufficient to support the claim.
Identify gaps, assumptions, or an alternative explanation the data could also support.
Aligns to selected NGSS SEP (Analyzing and Interpreting Data)
Phase 3
CER Written Response
10 min, independent
Write a Claim, Evidence, Reasoning response to a prompt about the text.
State your claim clearly, cite specific evidence, and explain the reasoning that connects them.
Use the Crosscutting Concept to strengthen your reasoning, not just your claim.
Aligns to selected NGSS SEP (Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions)
Phase 4
Argumentation & Application
10 min, small group then whole class
Defend your CER response to a partner or small group.
Respond to a counterargument or an alternative explanation from a peer.
Connect the science to a real engineering or design problem, or to the DEEDS challenge.
Aligns to selected NGSS SEP (Engaging in Argument from Evidence)

Why this, why now
This routine builds scientific thinking: making a claim, weighing evidence, and reasoning through a Crosscutting Concept or Science and Engineering Practice. Younger students build these habits through a shared text and repeated reading; older students build them through independent close reading and a defensible Claim-Evidence-Reasoning argument. Reading skill develops alongside that thinking, not instead of it.

The Argumentation phase (6-12) asks students to defend a claim and respond to a counterargument. Structured discussion ranks among the highest-impact practices in Hattie's research.
Selecting the CCC or SEP
  • Choose the Crosscutting Concept before you select the text. The CCC should already fit the unit. This routine gives students a second, low-stakes chance to notice it in a new context.
  • The Science and Engineering Practice in each phase should match whatever your core curriculum is targeting that week.
  • Name the CCC or SEP explicitly to students before the phase that uses it. Students should know what they're looking for, not guess at it.
Facilitation Notes: K–5
  • The repeated reading exists to make the claim or Crosscutting Concept visible to students, not just to build fluency. Don't cut it under time pressure.
  • Keep the claim in Phase 1 concrete. "The author says ___" is a fine claim at this age. Save nuance for 6-12.
Facilitation Notes: 6–12
  • Independent close reading is what lets students build their own claim, not a shared one. Resist the urge to add repeated choral reading back in.
  • Phase 2 is where students most often default to summary instead of evaluation. Push them to name a specific gap or an alternative explanation, not just restate the data.
  • Phase 4's argumentation only works if students have genuinely different claims or reasoning to defend. If everyone reaches the same CER response, the discussion has nothing to argue about.
Text Selection & Length
  • K–2: up to 150 words   3–8: up to 250 words   9–12: up to 300 words
  • Choose texts with a clear claim and enough data or observation for students to evaluate the evidence behind it, not just a description of a phenomenon.
  • The Engineering Case Study in Phase 4 needs an actual design constraint or tradeoff for learners to reason through. A single correct answer with no tradeoff will not sustain the phase.
