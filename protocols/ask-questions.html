← Examine EXAMINE A.S.K. Questions ⏱ 15 min
💡 Use this after you've explored the challenge to turn your curiosity into questions you can actually investigate in Examine.
A
Ask

With your group, write as many questions as you can about the challenge. Don't judge yet, just get your ideas down.

Helpful starters:

  • How does ___ affect ___?
  • What makes ___ happen or change?
  • Why do people experience ___ differently?
  • What would happen if ___?
  • How could ___ be improved?
S
Sort

Group your questions by type:

  • Cause: Why does this happen?
  • Impact: What would happen if?
  • System: How is this set up or maintained?
  • Perspective: Why do people see or experience this differently?
  • Solution: How could this change?
  • Process: How could we...?

If a type is missing, add a new question.

K
Keep

Check each question against the criteria below. Choose the 3-5 best questions to keep.

  • Relevant: Connects clearly to the challenge
  • Investigable: Can be explored with evidence (data, texts, interviews, observations)
  • Deep: Goes beyond a quick fact or yes/no answer
Standards alignment: CCSS.ELA-Literacy.CCRA.W.7 (conduct research projects based on focused questions), NGSS Science and Engineering Practice: Asking Questions and Defining Problems.

Why this, why now
A.S.K. (Ask, Sort, Keep) helps students turn curiosity into purposeful inquiry after they've explored the challenge through experiences, perspectives, and data in Discover. It gives them practice asking investigable questions, prioritizing the ones that matter most, and preparing to seek answers to them across Examine.

Sorting questions by cause, impact, system, and perspective organizes ideas around structures, not disconnected facts.

When students choose which questions to investigate and explain why, learning feels purposeful and motivating.
Metacognitive
Thinking
Evaluating their own questions
The Keep step asks students to judge the quality of their own questions against explicit criteria.
Facilitation Notes
  • Run this after students have already explored the challenge through an immersive experience, multiple perspectives, and data, not before. A.S.K. is a synthesis move, not an entry point.
  • In the Ask step, protect the no-judgment brainstorm. Redirecting or correcting a question too early will shrink the pool students have to sort from.
  • If a Sort category comes back empty, that's useful information. It may point to an angle the class hasn't considered yet, not just a gap to fill for its own sake.
  • The 3-5 questions students keep should carry directly into their Examine investigation. If a kept question doesn't connect to what they'll actually research, revisit it.
