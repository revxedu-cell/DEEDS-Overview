The Share Experience — three components every scholar completes
Explanation of Learning
Scholars explain what they worked on, how their thinking developed, and why key decisions were made. May be individual or team, live or recorded, short or extended.
Dialogue
Scholars engage with questions that press on reasoning, collaboration, tradeoffs, and consequences — justifying decisions using evidence and acknowledging limits.
Reflection on Growth & Impact
Scholars reflect on Intellectual Prowess, Strong Sense of Self, and Creating Impact — naming how their thinking, identity, and work in the world have grown.
Share can be structured as a single-day event, a multi-day block, or self-scheduled defenses. See the Facilitator Guide for format options.
By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Share, students should demonstrate growth in three areas of public communication and reflection:

1

Communicate Learning

Explain what was learned and done clearly to an audience outside the classroom, referencing evidence from across the full project to support explanations.

CCSS SL.4–6 · NGSS SEP 6, SEP 8 · C3 D4.3
2

Reflect on Growth

Reflect on how thinking, skills, and sense of self developed across the project — using specific examples to explain what changed and why it matters.

CCSS SL.1, SL.4 · NGSS SEP 7
3

Engage with Your Audience

Respond thoughtfully to questions, critique, or feedback — clarifying, defending, or building on ideas when challenged by audience members.

CCSS SL.1, SL.3 · C3 D4.6-8 · NGSS SEP 6

Evidence of Learning

End of Phase

These prompts identify the core thinking students should demonstrate before moving forward. Teachers may adapt the language, scaffolds, and response format while preserving the underlying thinking.

Students are ready to complete the phase when they can respond to the following:

  1. What did you learn, create, and do across the DEEDS process?
  2. What evidence supports your key decisions and conclusions?
  3. How did you grow, what impact did the work have, and what questions remain?

Students may respond through discussion, writing, drawing, models, demonstrations, presentations, or other formats appropriate to their age, the learning goals, and the project context.
