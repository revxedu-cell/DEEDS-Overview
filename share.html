Share: Phase 5 of DEEDS

Where learning goes public.

Students make their learning visible, explaining what they investigated, what they built, and what they changed, to a real audience beyond the classroom.

Student Experience

~2–3 sessions

Share creates a high-impact opportunity for students to make their learning public, reflect on their growth, and contribute their thinking to an audience beyond the classroom.

Share with Your Audience
Three components every student completes
Explanation of Learning: explain what they worked on, how their thinking developed, and why key decisions were made
Dialogue: engage with questions that press on reasoning, tradeoffs, and consequences, justifying decisions using evidence
Reflection on Growth & Impact: reflect on Intellectual Prowess, Strong Sense of Self, and Creating Impact

Format Options
Single-Day Event
All students share in one block. Works well for community events, exhibitions, and short timelines.
Multi-Day Block
A subset shares each day. Non-presenting students observe, give feedback, or refine reflections. Good for large classes and deeper dialogue.
Self-Scheduled Defenses
Students sign up for a defense slot. Each includes explanation, dialogue, and reflection. Works well for secondary grades and capstones.
Also in This Phase
Final Summative Assessment
Students demonstrate mastery of the disciplinary content addressed across the module. Typically a traditional assessment administered separately from the Share event.
By the End

Success Criteria

By the end of Share, students should demonstrate growth in three areas of public communication and reflection:

1

Communicate Learning

Explain what was learned and done clearly to an audience outside the classroom, referencing evidence from across the full project to support explanations.

CCSS SL.4–6 · NGSS SEP 6, SEP 8 · C3 D4.3
2

Reflect on Growth

Reflect on how thinking, skills, and sense of self developed across the project, using specific examples to explain what changed and why it matters.

CCSS SL.1, SL.4 · NGSS SEP 7
3

Engage with Your Audience

Respond thoughtfully to questions, critique, or feedback, clarifying, defending, or building on ideas when challenged by audience members.

CCSS SL.1, SL.3 · C3 D4.6-8 · NGSS SEP 6
Your Role in Share

Teacher Guidance

Share is the culmination of the whole project. Your job is to facilitate an experience worthy of the work students have done, one where they communicate their learning to an audience beyond the classroom, field genuine questions, and reflect on who they've become through the process.

  • Identify and confirm the audience for the Share event: community partners, caregivers, school leaders, or others connected to the challenge.
  • Choose a format that fits the project and audience (exhibition, presentation, panel, community meeting).
  • Plan time for Project Rewind and drafting before students rehearse.
  • Prepare scaffolds to support students in structuring their Explanation of Learning.
  • Build in a celebration of effort, growth, and impact.
  • Run Project Rewind early in the phase to help students synthesize their learning across all phases.
  • Give students structured time to draft, then use T.A.G. Feedback to sharpen their explanations before rehearsal.
  • Coach students to reflect on and explain how their thinking has evolved throughout the experience.
  • Prepare students for audience questions, including critical ones, through rehearsal.
  • After the Share event, use the Final Growth Reflection to help students name what changed in their thinking and sense of self across the project.
