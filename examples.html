Creating Safer Intersections in the Bronx
Fourth Grade Module · Days 1–30 · Co-designed with LEAD359 and RevX
Challenge Question How can we design and promote effective ways to make intersections safer for pedestrians and the community? 3-5-ETS1-1 · 4-PS3-1 · 4-PS3-3 · 4-PS4-1 · 4-PS4-3
Discover Days 1–3

Learners took part in a walk audit near their school, observing driver behavior and intersection conditions. Experts from Transportation Alternatives and the Center for Architecture helped students understand why intersections needed redesign for pedestrian safety.

Examine Days 4–14

Students investigated how speeding impacts pedestrian safety through crash simulations and real-world traffic data analysis, building a conceptual understanding of energy transfer through explicit NGSS instruction.

Engineer Days 15–24

Working with Center for Architecture mentors, students crafted detailed 3D models of safer intersections and built companion websites explaining the science behind their designs.

Do Days 25–28

Learners launched their websites and tracked petition signatures as support for the NYC Streets Plan grew, measuring real civic impact from fourth graders.

Share Days 29–30

Students reflected on their roles as Transportation Engineers, sharing their growth and celebrating the connection between their science learning and real community change.

The Future of Fashion is Already in Your Closet
Fast Fashion & Consumption · Grade 6 DEEDS Unit · Days 1–32
Challenge Question How can an upcycled fashion brand prove that style doesn't require buying new clothes? MS-PS1-1 · MS-PS1-2 · MS-PS1-3 · MS-ESS3-3 · MS-ESS3-4
Discover Days 1–5

Students met with FabScrap, a nonprofit textile recycling warehouse, and analyzed data on the environmental, labor, and public health impacts of fast fashion, then collected donated clothing as raw materials.

Examine Days 6–21

Learners conducted scientific investigations into materials science and fabric chemistry — dyeing, bleaching, shrinking, fraying — and wrote CER arguments recommending materials for real design scenarios.

Engineer Days 22–27

Working alongside fashion design grad students, learners constructed upcycled garments from donated clothing, applying materials science while preparing pieces for the runway.

Do Days 28–29

Students launched their runway show for peers, families, and community members, then collected data on how audience attitudes toward fashion shifted.

Share Days 30–32

Learners reflected on their identities as materials scientists and sustainable designers, completing a summative science assessment celebrating real-world impact.

Mental Health for Everyone
Eleventh Grade Module · Days 1–30 · Co-designed with McComb School District (MS) and RevX
Challenge Question How might we design campaigns to increase mental health awareness in our community? RI 11.1 · RI 11.4 · RI 11.6
Discover Days 1–3

Students named mental health as a community issue through a gallery walk, videos, and trend data, then connected with NAMI Mississippi, whose Let's Talk 988 session introduced the crisis hotline.

Examine Days 4–14

Students researched Mississippi's strained mental health system, wrote informative reports, and identified target audiences — student athletes, college-bound seniors, and teens affected by social media.

Engineer Days 15–24

Learners designed multi-modal campaign materials — videos, flyers, and social media content — each group developing a distinct approach for its target audience.

Do Days 24–30

Students hosted events across the county, including a session for athletes on anxiety and pressure in sports. Over 100 students attended and were surveyed before and after.

Share Days 28–30

Learners shared their work directly with school board members — an act of civic leadership that led to multiple district-sponsored mental health events across the community.
